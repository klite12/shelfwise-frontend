Grammarly

Quick summary

Grammarly checks your writing wherever you type — a browser extension, a desktop app, and plugins for Word and Google Docs among others. The free plan handles basic spelling, grammar, and tone detection with no credit card required, while Grammarly Pro unlocks full-sentence rewrites, deeper clarity suggestions, plagiarism detection, and a much larger monthly allowance of AI prompts.

Who it's for

Good fit

  • Students and professionals who mainly need typo and grammar catches
  • Anyone writing daily emails, docs, or social posts who wants a second pair of eyes
  • Teams wanting a shared style guide and consistent tone across writers

Not a fit

  • Long-form authors and editors who want deep, structural manuscript feedback — dedicated tools go further there
  • Anyone uneasy about a writing tool reading their drafts, especially for sensitive documents
  • People who only need occasional checks — the free tier already covers that without upgrading

What's in each plan

  • Free: spelling, grammar, and basic tone detection, plus 100 AI prompts a month
  • Pro: full-sentence rewrites, plagiarism detection, deeper clarity and word-choice suggestions, 2,000 AI prompts a month
  • Works across the browser, desktop app, Gmail, Outlook, Slack, and Word
  • Light team features on Pro — shared style guide, brand tone, snippets, team analytics
  • Enterprise: SSO, admin controls, and contractual compliance options for larger organizations

Pricing

Grammarly's free plan costs nothing and needs no card on file. Pro runs about $12 per month if billed annually (roughly $144/year), or around $30 per month if you'd rather pay monthly with no commitment — a fairly steep jump for going month-to-month, so it's worth deciding upfront how long you'll actually use it. Business and Enterprise plans use custom, per-seat pricing negotiated directly with Grammarly.

Pros and cons

Pros
  • Free tier is genuinely usable long-term, not a crippled trial
  • Works nearly everywhere you type, not just one app
  • Rewrite and tone-adjustment features on Pro save real editing time for daily writers
  • Well-established, widely reviewed product with a large, stable user base
Cons
  • Suggestions can occasionally feel overly aggressive or miss the intended tone
  • Monthly billing is noticeably more expensive than annual — easy to overpay if you forget to switch
  • Auto-renews at standard pricing after any promotional period, so it's worth calendaring a reminder

Should you upgrade to Pro?

If you mainly need typo-catching for essays, emails, or social posts, the free plan already does that job — there's little reason to pay just for basic checking. Pro earns its cost when you're writing daily and want the tool to actually rewrite awkward sentences or check for plagiarism, rather than just flag errors and leave the fixing to you. If you're unsure, start free and only upgrade once you find yourself hitting the 100-prompt monthly ceiling regularly.

Final verdict

A free tier worth using, a Pro tier worth waiting to need

Grammarly earns its high rating by making the free plan genuinely useful rather than a bait-and-switch trial. Most casual writers can stop there. Pro is a legitimate upgrade for people writing daily who lean on rewrites and tone adjustment — just budget for annual billing rather than the much pricier monthly rate.
